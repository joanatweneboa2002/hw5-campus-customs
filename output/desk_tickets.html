Reflection

I pulled this from the audit trail, my ticket tabs and the Cash tab. All 3 tickets took 22 agent steps and 139,028 tokens, and checking went from $3,400 to $2,560.

1 · How I'd grade the agents

TicketGradeWhy
103 Bulk hoodieA This one went exactly how I planned, in 5 steps. It only quoted the 8 size M hoodies we actually have, not all 20, at $52.20 each ($417.60 total). That's 10% off, so it stayed under the 15% limit and way above the $22 cost. The reply didn't give away our cost either. I wouldn't change anything.
101 Bulldog teeB It got the right answer but took the long way. Inventory sent the ticket to Customer Service instead of Accounting, so Boss had to step in and send it to Accounting after. The reply to Tauhid was honest and didn't promise a date, and the $840 for invoice #501 only got paid after I approved it. My one problem: Accounting never noticed the $2,400 rent coming up, and that was the whole reason cash was tight.
102 Rent dueC The real work was good. Facilities checked that Elm City Properties was the actual landlord, the $2,400 came from the lease and not the email, and Accounting flagged that paying it would leave us with $160. Then Boss and Accounting kept telling each other "still pending, keep monitoring" until the 10 step limit cut them off, and I had to run it again to close it. That loop alone burned 36,273 tokens.

So I got the agents and tools right every time. What I didn't predict was the order they'd hand things off in, and that nobody would know what to do while a payment was just sitting there waiting for me.

3 · What would've been simpler with one agent + tools

  • A lot fewer tokens. Boss used 57,737 of the 139,028 tokens (42%) just reading tickets and passing them along. One agent with all 10 tools could probably do 103 in 1 or 2 turns instead of 5.
  • No rereading. get_ticket got called 23 times in 22 steps because every agent has to reread the ticket to catch up. One agent would already know what's going on.
  • No 102 loop. With nobody to hand off to, it would just request the $2,400 and stop. That saves the 36,273 tokens and the rerun.
  • No detours like Inventory sending 101 to Customer Service first.
  • What I'd give up: right now each agent only gets the tools it needs. Customer Service can't request payments and Facilities can't make quotes. One agent would have everything. But the money is already protected by the tools and the approve button, since no tool can actually pay anything and quotes can't go below cost. So for these 3 tickets, one agent would've been fine.
